2. The workspace

Your workspace runs on a server controlled by its hosting organization, whose own workplace policies also apply to you. Access is by invitation or account from that organization: use only workspaces you are authorized to use, keep your sign-in details to yourself, and tell your workspace administrator if you suspect unauthorized access.

3. Your responsibilities

  • Share only content you have the right to share, including the right to store it on the workspace's server and show it to the room's members.
  • Remember that messages, including direct messages, and uploaded files are stored on the workspace's server, may remain in backups, and can be accessible to the people operating it. Do not treat workspace chat as private from the hosting organization.
  • When you connect Google Calendar or share a Drive file, you grant the Google permissions shown in Google's own consent screens. Those grants are yours: understand who receives access before you confirm.
  • Do not misuse the service: no unauthorized access attempts, no interference with other members' use, no unlawful content, and no use of integrations to harm other systems.

4. Content rights

You and your organization keep your rights in content you post; nothing here assigns them to the people who build Smartfire. By posting, you allow the hosting organization to store, back up, and display that content to workspace members as needed to run the workspace. Employment or contractor agreements with your organization may separately govern who owns work-related content.

5. Third-party services

Workspaces can connect to services run by others, such as Google (sign-in, Calendar, Drive), GitHub, and the workspace's real-time media provider. Your use of those services follows their own terms, including Google's terms for Google services. Integrations are provided as available and may change or stop working; neither the software's authors nor, unless they separately promise it, the hosting organization guarantees any integration or service level.

6. Access changes and removal

The hosting organization may suspend or remove access, and may remove content or accounts, to operate its workspace or meet its obligations. Removed content may persist in backups or where the organization must keep it for business or legal reasons. See the privacy policy for how data retention works.

This workspace is operated by the organization hosting it. Your workspace administrator can tell you who that is.

Direct questions about these terms to your workspace administrator.
